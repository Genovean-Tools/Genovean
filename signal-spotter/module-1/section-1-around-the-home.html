Ealry Signs: Around the Home

The home often shows the earliest signs of change. This section helps you look honestly at what you have already noticed, before you explain it away.

Around the Home
1 Dishes, laundry, or basic cleaning being left undone for days Not reviewed

You walk into the kitchen and there are dishes from two days ago. The laundry basket is overflowing in a way it never used to be. The bathroom has not been cleaned in a way you can feel. You are not looking for a spotless home. You are looking for a shift from what was normal for this person. Someone who always kept a clean home letting things go is a different signal entirely from someone who was always more relaxed about it.

What to look for

Think about their baseline. How did their home usually look six months ago? A year ago? Compare that to what you see now. Look at the kitchen sink, the bathroom surfaces, and the laundry. These are the three areas that most reliably show a change in daily functioning. You are not inspecting. You are noticing.

How to observe without making it an issue

If you visit regularly, changes are easy to miss because they happen gradually. Try to think about your last three visits as a sequence. Has it been getting worse? If you have not visited in a while, notice the feeling when you walk in the door before you start talking. That first impression is often the most honest signal you will get.

Example of a useful note

"Visited on a Tuesday in April. Dishes in the sink from what looked like two or three days. Laundry piled on the bedroom chair. She said she had not been feeling well but brushed it off. Same thing the week before. This used to never happen."

Why this note matters. The details you write here, the specific day, the specific thing you noticed, the way it made you feel, become your evidence when you need to have a conversation with a sibling, a doctor, or your parent. A vague memory fades. A written observation does not.
2 Rooms or areas of the home becoming cluttered or unsafe Not reviewed

Piles of mail, newspapers, or objects building up in places they never used to accumulate. A spare room slowly becoming storage. Pathways through the home getting narrower. Boxes or bags that have been sitting on the floor for weeks. This is not about tidiness as a preference, it is about whether the home is starting to feel like it is getting away from them. Clutter that builds without being addressed often signals that energy, motivation, or cognitive capacity for managing space has quietly declined.

What to look for

Walk through the home with fresh eyes. Look at the hallway, the kitchen counter, the area around their chair or bed. Are there items that have clearly been there for weeks without moving? Are there fall hazards, things on the floor, loose rugs, objects in walkways? Notice whether this is new or whether it has always been their style.

How to observe without making it an issue

Do not start moving things or commenting. Just look and note. If the clutter feels unsafe, things that could cause a fall, items left on stairs, cords across walkways, that moves from observation to action. But for now, your job is to record what you see, not to fix it today.

Example of a useful note

"The hallway has a rug that has been bunched up near the entrance for about a month. Three bags of shopping near the back door that have not been unpacked. The living room is fine but the kitchen counter is covered in unopened envelopes and newspapers going back several weeks."

Why this note matters. Clutter that builds is easy to normalise when you visit regularly. Writing it down with a date makes the pattern visible. If you come back in six weeks and it is worse, you have a before and after. That matters when you need to explain what you have been seeing.
3 Home maintenance being ignored or left unaddressed Not reviewed

A light that has been out for months. A tap that drips but has not been fixed. A garden that used to be tended now looking overgrown. Gutters full, paintwork peeling, something broken that has been left. These are not necessarily signs of laziness or not caring, they are often signs that the cognitive or physical energy required to manage and coordinate home repairs has become more than the person can easily manage. What used to get handled quietly is now sitting undone.

What to look for

Think about what they used to maintain and whether it is still being maintained. The garden, the exterior of the home, small repairs inside. Ask yourself: would this have been left like this a year ago? If the answer is no, it is worth noting. Pay attention to anything that is a safety concern, a broken step, a faulty lock, a gas smell that is being dismissed.

How to observe without making it an issue

A gentle way to open this is to offer to help with something specific rather than raising concern directly. "Would you like me to take a look at that tap while I am here?" is not an accusation. It gives you access and it gives them help without the conversation needing to be about what you have noticed.

Example of a useful note

"The back gutters have been blocked since autumn and he keeps saying he will get someone in. The garden gate latch is broken and has been since February. He used to sort things like this immediately. Now they just sit. He seems not to notice or not to care."

Why this note matters. Maintenance that goes undone has a compounding effect. Small things become bigger things. And the pattern, things being left that would not have been left before, is itself important information about where they are right now.
4 The home feeling different in a way that is hard to name Not reviewed

Sometimes the sign is not one specific thing. It is a feeling you get when you walk through the door. The home feels quieter than it used to. Less alive. Curtains that are not being opened. A chair in a room that is no longer used. Smaller signs of daily life, the kettle being used, the newspaper being read, the radio being on, that seem to have become less frequent or less present. You cannot always point to one thing, but something has shifted and you feel it before you can explain it.

What to look for

Trust the feeling, then try to find specific evidence for it. What is the home missing that it used to have? What is different from six months ago? It might be as subtle as the blinds always being closed now, or the kitchen not smelling like cooking the way it used to. Look for the small routines of daily life and notice which ones have gone quiet.

How to observe without making it an issue

You do not need to name this feeling to anyone yet. Your job in this section is to put it into words for yourself. Writing down a vague feeling and giving it specific details, what time of day, what you noticed, what was missing, turns it from a worry into an observation. Observations are something you can work with.

Your notes

Example of a useful note

"Hard to put my finger on it but the flat feels different. The sitting room curtains are always closed now even in the afternoon. She used to have Radio 4 on constantly. The kitchen feels like it is being used less. It is not dirty, it just feels quieter than it used to."

Why this note matters. The hardest signals to name are often the most important ones. If you write this down now, in your own words, you will be able to look back and see exactly when you first felt something had changed. That is information no one else can give you.
