Moving into Module 2

You have worked through what you have noticed in daily life, around the home, around food, and around how your parent is getting around. Module 2 does something different. It asks you to step back from the individual observations and look at what they mean together. A single thing noticed once is an observation. The same thing noticed again and again, across different visits and different situations, is a pattern. This module helps you tell the difference -- and then helps you understand what that pattern is telling you.

What You Are Seeing
1 The same thing has happened more than once, and you noticed Not reviewed

There is a specific thing, a behaviour, a lapse, a change in how they look or speak or manage, that has now happened on more than one occasion. You noticed it the first time and put it to one side. You noticed it again and felt something shift. That shift -- from a single observation to a recurring one -- is what this signal is about. It does not matter what the specific thing is yet. What matters is that it has happened more than once and that you are here, which means part of you already suspects it will happen again. That suspicion is not anxiety. It is pattern recognition, and it is worth taking seriously.

What to look for

Think about the one thing from Module 1 that has stayed with you the most. The observation that comes back to you when you are not thinking about it. Now ask yourself: has it happened again since you first noticed it? Has it happened more than once in the last month, the last three months, the last six months? You are not counting incidents to reach a threshold. You are asking yourself honestly whether this is something that keeps showing up.

How to work through this honestly

It is common at this stage to want to qualify everything. To say: yes, but it was a bad week. Yes, but they were tired. Yes, but it has not been that bad lately. Those qualifications are natural. Write them down -- but write down the thing itself first, before the qualifications. The thing that keeps happening is the signal. The explanations for it are the noise.

How would you describe this signal?

Your notes

Example of a useful note

"The thing that keeps coming back to me is the bills. I noticed it in January, then again in March when the electricity company sent a second notice. Then again in May when I found three months of bank statements still in their envelopes on the kitchen counter. Each time I explained it differently. Looking at them together now I cannot explain them differently anymore. This is not a bad few weeks. This is a pattern."

Why this note matters. Naming the repeating thing is the hardest step in this entire module. Once it is written down in plain language, you cannot unsee it. That is not a bad thing. It is the beginning of being able to do something about it.
2 It is happening across more than one area of their life Not reviewed

A single signal in one area of daily life could be explained by circumstance, stress, illness, a difficult period. When similar signals appear across multiple areas at the same time, that explanation becomes harder to sustain. The home is less cared for and the food situation is different and they are going out less. Or the memory concerns are appearing alongside a change in mood and a change in how they manage their money. When change clusters across areas of life rather than appearing in isolation, it is telling you something more significant than any individual signal can. This is one of the most important things to see clearly, and one of the easiest to miss.

What to look for

Look back at what you recorded in Module 1. Identify the areas where you marked something, any of the four frequency options, not just "happening regularly." Now ask: are these connected? Are they clustered in time -- did several things start shifting around the same period? Are they related in cause -- could a single underlying change explain more than one of the things you have noticed? Clustering and timing are the two indicators that transform isolated signals into a pattern worth understanding.

How to work through this honestly

Resist the temptation to keep the signals separate. It is easier to manage one concern at a time. But if several things are shifting simultaneously, keeping them in separate mental boxes means you are underestimating what you are seeing. This note is the place to put them in the same box for the first time and look at what that actually tells you.

Your notes

Example of a useful note

"Looking at it together for the first time: the home has been harder to keep, food has become simpler, she is going out less, she seems flatter emotionally, and the bills situation. All of this started becoming visible around the same time -- roughly six months ago, after the fall in autumn. I have been treating each of these as separate things. Written together like this they are not separate at all."

Why this note matters. This note is the first time in this course that you are putting the full picture together rather than looking at individual pieces of it. What you write here will inform everything in the sections that follow. Be as honest and complete as you can.
3 This is different from how they have always been Not reviewed

This signal matters more than any other in this section, and it is the one most likely to be overlooked. Not because it is subtle, but because it requires you to hold two images of the same person in your mind at the same time. The person they are now, and the person they were. A parent who has always been disorganised not managing their bills is not the same signal as a parent who was meticulous with money for forty years now letting bills pile up unopened. A parent who was always quiet and solitary withdrawing is not the same signal as a parent who was social and active no longer leaving the house. The change only becomes a signal when it is understood against the baseline of who this person actually was. Without that comparison, what you are seeing is incomplete.

What to look for

Think about the person your parent was two years ago. Three years ago. What were their habits? What did they care about maintaining? What were they known for, by themselves, by others? Now compare that person to the person you have been observing in Module 1 and in the signals above. The gap between those two versions is the signal. The bigger that gap, the more significant what you are seeing becomes.

How to work through this honestly

Be careful not to set the baseline too high. You are not comparing them to their best, most capable self. You are comparing them to who they consistently were, the ordinary version of the person you have known for most of your life. Small, consistent shifts from that ordinary version are more meaningful than dramatic departures from an idealised memory.

How would you describe this signal?

Your notes

Example of a useful note

"He was always the person who had everything sorted. Car serviced on the dot, bills filed, garden immaculate. His home was something he was genuinely proud of. What I am seeing now is so far from that person that it is hard to hold them as the same man. The gap is not small. It is significant. And I have been explaining it away for over a year."

Why this note matters. This is the most important note in Module 2. The baseline you write here, who they were, is what every other observation in this course is measured against. Return to it. Update it. It is the foundation of everything that follows.
